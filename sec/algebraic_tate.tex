\section{The algebraic Tate diagonal}\label{Tatediag}

Let \(V\) be a chain complex in \(\cD(\Fp)\).

\subsection{The algebraic Tate diagonal}

\begin{definition}
	We denote by \(T_p\) the composite
	\[
	\cD(\Fp) \xra{- \otimes \overset{p}{\cdots} \otimes -} \cD(\Fp[\cyc_p]) \xra{(-)^{t\cyc_p}} \cD(\Fp).
	\]
	Explicitly, \(T_pV = (V^{\ot p})^{t\cyc_p}\) is the \(\cyc_p\)-Tate construction of the chain complex of \(\Fp[\cyc_p]\)-modules \(V^{\otimes p}\), whose \(\cyc_p\)-action is given by cyclically permuting the factors.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}
	Evaluating in \(V = \Fp\), we obtain \(T_p(\Fp) = (\Fp^{\otimes p})^{t\cyc_p} \cong \Fp^{t\cyc_p}\cong \bigoplus_{i} \Fp\{\widehat e_i\}\).
	Also, observe that if we take a general suspension \(V = \Fp[n]\), then 
	\[
	T_p(\Fp[n]) = (\Fp[n]^{\ot p})^{t\cyc_p} \cong (\Fp[pn])^{t\cyc_p} \cong \bigoplus_{i} \Fp\{\widehat e_i\}[pn] \cong T_p(\Fp).\martin{I don't know if the general suspension case is useful apart from being a curiosity of the case \(\Fp\). It may confuse the reader.}
	\]
\end{example}

We now describe a generator of such \(\Fp\)-vector space.
Let us fix an isomorphism \(\varphi \colon H(\widehat W_p \ot_{\cyc_p} V^{\ot p}) \xra{\cong} H(T_pV)\).

\begin{definition}
	Let \(\Delta^t_p\) be the natural transformation
	\[
	\Delta^t_p \colon \id_{\cD(\Fp)} \to T_p
	\]
	which is characterised on homology by
	\[
	\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=0, column sep=small]
		(\Delta^t_pV)_* &[-20pt] \colon H(V) \arrow[r] & H(T_pV) \\
		& v \arrow[r, |->] & \varphi\left(\widehat{e}_{(1-p)|v|} \ot (v \ot \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot v)\right).
	\end{tikzcd}
	\]
\end{definition}

We will often ommit the isomorphism \(\varphi\) for clarity of notation, and denote the components simply by \(\Delta^t_p\) instead of \(\Delta^t_pV\).
Observe that \(\Delta^t_p\) is nonzero.
In fact, evaluated at \(\Fp\) corresponds to the inclusion \(\Fp \hookrightarrow T_p(\Fp)\) given by \(1 \mapsto \widehat e_0\).
To conclude that \(\Delta^t_p\) is indeed an algebraic Tate diagonal, it remains to prove its linearity.

\begin{lemma}\label{linearity}
	\(\Delta^t_p\) is \(\bF_p\)-linear.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	First we check scalar multiplicativity, let \(a \in \bF_p\) and \(x \in H_n(V)\).
	\[
	\Delta^t_p(ax) = \widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot (ax)^{\ot p} = a^p(\widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot x^{\ot p}) = a\Delta^t_p(x),
	\]
	where the last equality holds in \(\bF_p\) due to Fermat's little theorem.
	Now, for additivity, let \(x\) and \(y \in H_n(V)\).
	We have
	\[
	\Delta^t_p(x+y) = \widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot (x+y)^{\ot p}.
	\]
	The term \((x+y)^{\ot p}\) is a sum of \(2^p\) terms, corresponding to the set of all possible words of length \(p\) using the letters \(x\) and \(y\).
	The cyclic group acts on this set by permutation, and there are two types of orbits: those consisting of a single element and those free with \(p\) elements.
	There are two trivial orbits with a single element, corresponding to \(x^{\otimes p}\) and \(y^{\otimes p}\) respectively.
	Let \({\bf w}_1,\dots,{\bf w}_{(2^p-2)/p}\) be representatives of the remaining orbits.
	Thus, we can write
	\[
	\widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot (x+y)^{\ot p} = \widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot x^{\ot p} + \widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot y^{\ot p} + \sum_{i=1}^{(2^p-2)/p} \widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot N{\bf w}_i,
	\]
	% 	but
	% 	\[
	% 	\widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot_{\cyc_p} N{\bf w}_i = N\widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot_{\cyc_p} {\bf w}_i = d\widehat e_{(1-p)n+1} \ot_{\cyc_p} {\bf w}_i = d(\widehat e_{(1-p)n+1} \ot_{\cyc_p} {\bf w}_i),
	% 	\]
	% 	where the second equality holds because \((1-p)n+1\) is an odd number for \(p\) prime (and \(d\widehat e_j = N\widehat e_{j-1}\) for any \(j\) if \(p=2\)), and the last equality holds since \(x\) and \(y\) are cycles.
	but the elements \(\widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot N{\bf w}_i\) lie on the subcomplex of \(\widehat W_p \ot H(V)^{\ot p}\) of free orbits, which is acyclic, meaning that the term \(\sum_{i=1}^{(2^p-2)/p} \widehat e_{(1-p)n} \ot N{\bf w}_i\) vanishes, and so
	\[
	\Delta^t_p(x+y) = \Delta^t_p(x) + \Delta^t_p(y).\qedhere
	\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
	Every algebraic Tate diagonal is of the form \(a\Delta^t_p\), for \(a \in \Fp^\times\).
\end{corollary}

This means that, up to non-zero scalar multiplication, \(\Delta^t_p\) is the only algebraic Tate diagonal, thus, we refer to it as \defn{the algebraic Tate diagonal}.

\begin{remark}
	Note that \(\Delta^t_p\) is not canonical, since it depends on the choice of the isomorphism \(\varphi\).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Properties of \(T_pV\)}

The image of \(HV\) via \((\Delta^t_p)_*\) generates \(H(T_pV)\) as a free \(\Fp[u,u^{-1}]\)-module with action
\[
u \Delta^t_*(v) = u(\widehat e_{(1-p)|v|} \ot v^{\ot p}) \coloneqq \widehat e_{(1-p)|v|+1} \ot v^{\ot p}.
\]
In other words, we have the following.

\begin{lemma}\label{pTate_iso}
	The map
	\[
	\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=0, column sep=small]
		\Fp[u,u^{-1}] \ot H(V) \arrow[r] & H(T_pV) \\
		\hspace*{13pt} u^k \ot v \arrow[r, |->] & u^k \Delta^t_*(v)
	\end{tikzcd}
	\]
	is an isomorphism of free \(\Fp[u,u^{-1}]\)-modules.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	First, we have that
	\[
	H(T_pV) \cong H(\widehat W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} V^{\otimes p}) \cong H(\widehat W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} H(V)^{\otimes p}). \martin{This first step is delicate, I am not fully sure that it is true without any extra assumptions. In particular, this implies that the spectral sequence in Lemma \cref{spectral_seq} converges at the second page for the case \(M = V^{\ot p}\), which should be true.}
	\]
	Now, \(H(V)\) is a graded \(\Fp\)-vector space, so we can consider a basis \(\set{v_\alpha}_{\alpha \in A}\) for \(H(V)\).
	Since \(H(V)^{\ot p}\) is a permutation module, we can decompose it into orbits.
	There are two types of orbits, the trivial orbits with a single element and the free orbits with \(p\) elements, that is,
	\[
	H(V)^{\ot p} \cong \left(\bigoplus_{\alpha \in A} \Fp\set{v_\alpha^{\ot p}}\right) \oplus \left(\bigoplus_{\text{free orbits}} \Fp[\cyc_p]\right).
	\]
	Tensoring over \(\cyc_p\) with \(\widehat W_p\) gives
	\[
	\widehat W_p \ot_{\cyc_p} H(V)^{\ot p} \cong \left(\bigoplus_{\alpha \in A} \widehat W_p \ot_{\cyc_p} \Fp\set{v_\alpha^{\ot p}}\right) \oplus \left(\bigoplus_{\text{free orbits}} \widehat W_p\right),
	\]
	but \(\widehat W_p\) is acyclic, and \(H(\widehat W_p \ot_{\cyc_p} \Fp) \cong H(T_p\Fp) \cong \Fp[u,u^{-1}]\).
	It is thus clear that the given map is an isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{fibre_cofibre}
	\(T_pV[-1] \cong T_pV\) in \(\cD(\Fp)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	The proof of \cref{pTate_iso} works analogously for \(H(T_pV[-1])\), which means that there are isomorphisms
	\[
	H(T_pV[-1]) \cong H(\widetilde W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} V^{\ot p}) \cong \Fp[u,u^{-1}] \ot H(V) \cong H(\widehat W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} V^{\ot p}) \cong H(T_pV).
	\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
	\(T_pV\) fits in the following fibre-cofibre sequence. % whats the appropiate name for this?
	\[
	\cdots \to (V^{\ot p})^{h\cyc_p}[-1] \to T_pV \to (V^{\ot p})_{h\cyc_p} \overset{Nm}\longrightarrow (V^{\ot p})^{h\cyc_p} \to T_pV \to \cdots
	\]
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Monoidality}

The identity functor \(\id_{\cD(\Fp)}\) is a strong monoidal functor: for chain complexes \(C\) and \(D\), there is a natural morphism \(\kappa\) in \(\cD(\Fp)\) that is characterised in homology by the Künneth isomorphism \martin{is this correctly formulated? Or rather \(\kappa\) is the identity, which is characterised in homology by the kunneth isomorphism? Thus the identity is monoidal, not lax monoidal. Anibal: The identity better be strong monoidal. }
\[
\kappa_* \colon H(C) \otimes H(D) \longrightarrow H(C \otimes D).
\]

\begin{proposition}
	The functor \(T_p\) admits a lax monoidal structure.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
	We can define the natural morphism characterised by
	\[
	\mu_* \circ \kappa_* \colon H\big(\widehat W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} C^{\otimes p}\big) \otimes H\big(\widehat W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} D^{\otimes p}\big) \longrightarrow H\big(\widehat W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} (C \otimes D)^{\otimes p}\big),
	\]
	where
	\[
	\mu_* \colon H\big((\widehat W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} C^{\otimes p}) \otimes (\widehat W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} D^{\otimes p})\big) \longrightarrow H\big(\widehat W_p \otimes_{\cyc_p} (C \otimes D)^{\otimes p}\big)
	\]
	is induced by the assignment
	\[
	[(\rho^a e_i \otimes \mathbf{c}) \otimes (\rho^b e_j \otimes \mathbf{d})] \longmapsto [e_{i+j} \otimes \mathrm{Sh}(\rho^{-a}\mathbf{c},\rho^{-b}\mathbf{d})],
	\]
	for \(\mathbf{c} = c_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes c_p \in C^{\otimes p}\) and \(\mathbf{d} = d_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes d_p \in D^{\otimes p}\).
	Here \(\mathrm{Sh}\) denotes the shuffle map \(C^{\otimes p} \times D^{\otimes p} \to (C \otimes D)^{\otimes p}\) given by
	\[
	((c_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes c_p),(d_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes d_p)) \longmapsto (c_1 \otimes d_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes (c_p \otimes d_p).
	\]
	Its is clear that the morphism is well defined and that it gives a lax monoidal structure for \(T_p\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
	The algebraic Tate diagonal is a monoidal natural transformation
	\[
	\Delta^t_p \colon \id_{\cD(\Fp)} \longrightarrow T_p
	\]
	with respect to the described lax monoidal structures.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	The following diagram commutes for all chain complexes \(C\) and \(D\).

	\begin{center}
		\begin{tikzpicture}
			\node (a) at (-3,2) {\(H(C) \otimes H(D)\)};
			\node (b) at (4,2) {\(H(\widehat W_p \otimes_{C_p} C^{\otimes p}) \otimes H(\widehat W_p \otimes_{C_p} D^{\otimes p})\)};
			\node (c) at (-3,0) {\(H(C \otimes D)\)};
			\node (d) at (4,0) {\(H(\widehat W_p \otimes_{C_p} (C \otimes D)^{\otimes p})\)};
			%
			\draw[->] (a) -- node[above] {\((\Delta^t_p)_* \otimes (\Delta^t_p)_*\)} (b);
			\draw[->] (a) -- node[right] {\(\kappa_*\)} (c);
			\draw[->] (b) -- node[right] {\(\mu_* \circ \kappa_*\)} (d);
			\draw[->] (c) -- node[above] {\((\Delta^t_p)_* \circ \otimes\)} (d);
		\end{tikzpicture}
	\end{center}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relation with the spectral Tate diagonal}

In this section we will show that the algebraic tate diagonal \(\Delta^t_p\) is not induced by the spectral Tate diagonal \(\Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) from \cref{dspectraltate}. Recall the map \(\can\) from \cref{HFpmod}. The composite
\[
\sH V\xra{\Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}} (\sH V \wedge_{\bS} \overset{p}{\cdots} \wedge_{\bS} \sH V)^{t\cyc_p} \xra{\can} \sH\left((V \ot_\Fp \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_\Fp V)^{t\cyc_p}\right) = \sH (T_pV)
\]
is a map in \(\mathsf{Sp}\) that is natural with respect to \(\sH\Fp\)-linear maps, and so, it induces an assignment \(V\to T_p V\), natural with respect to \(\Fp\)-linear maps.
It is reasonable to ask whether this assignment is an algebraic Tate diagonal, that is, if it is an actual nonzero map in \(\cD(\Fp)\).

\begin{proposition}\label{yesgo}
	\(\can \circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) does not induce an algebraic Tate diagonal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
	We argue by contradiction.
	If the statement was false, then there would exist a scalar \(a\in \Fp^\times\) such that \(\can \circ a\Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}} = \sH(\Delta^t_p)\) as natural maps in \(\sH\Fp\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}\). 
	By \cref{DFpclassification}, this is equivalent to saying that the left square in the following diagram commutes. 
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tikzpicture}
			\node (HF1) at (-5,1){\(\sH \Fp\)};
			\node (HF2) at (-5,-1){\(\sH \Fp\)};
			\node (THFF) at (0,1){\((\sH \Fp \wedge_{\bS} \overset{p}{\cdots} \wedge_{\bS} \sH \Fp)^{t\cyc_p}\)};
			\node (HFFT) at (0,-1){\(\sH\left((\Fp \ot_\Fp \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_\Fp \Fp)^{t\cyc_p}\right)\)};
			\node (HFT1) at (5,1){\((\sH \Fp)^{t\cyc_p}\)};
			\node (HFT2) at (5,-1){\(\sH (\Fp^{t\cyc_p})\)};
			%
			\draw[->] (HF1)--node[above]{\(a\Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\)}(THFF);
			\draw[->] (HF2)--node[above]{\(\sH(\Delta_p^t)\)}(HFFT);
			\draw[->] (THFF)--node[above]{\((m_{\sH \Fp}^{\mathsf{Sp}})^{t\cyc_p}\)}(HFT1);
			\draw[->](HFFT)--node[above]{\(\sH \left ((m_{\Fp})^{t\cyc_p}\right )\)}(HFT2);
			\draw[double, double distance=2pt](HF1)--(HF2);
			\draw[->](THFF)--node[right]{\(\can\)}(HFFT);
			\draw[->](HFT1)--node[right]{\(\cong\)}(HFT2);
		\end{tikzpicture}
	\end{center}
	The right square commutes, as we showed in \cref{rcan}, which then implies that the top and bottom compositions coincide.
	
	However, the upper row is a multiple of the Frobenius \(\varphi_{\sH \Fp}\) from [Nikolaus-Scholze, \S{IV.1}], and a consequence of [Nikolaus-Scholze, Theorem IV.1.15] is that it is not \(\sH \Fp\)-linear.
	This contradicts the fact that the lower row is \(\sH \Fp\)-linear, which means that the left square cannot commute.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
	Theorem III.1.10 in [Nikolaus-Scholze] states the following.\martin{what is the right way of quoting the theorem if we are about to say that it is not yet a theorem?}
	\begin{theorem}
		Every natural transformation \(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ} \colon C \to (C\ot_{\bZ} \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_{\bZ} C)^{t\cyc_p}\) of functors \(\cD(\bZ)\to\cD(\bZ)\) induces the zero map in homology.
	\end{theorem}
	Observe that the existence of algebraic Tate diagonals is a counterexample of this no-go theorem in \(\cD(\Fp)\).
	Yet, the proof of the theorem translates directly to \(\cD(\Fp)\).
	This is only possible because there is a gap in the proof, which was pointed out to the author by Federico Cantero Morán.
	
	In the proof they claim that for every such natural transformation \(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ}\), the underlying transformation of spectra factors as \(\can^{\bZ}\circ a \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\), for some \(a\in\Fp\).
	This is a crucial step, since it is later concluded that the scalar \(a\) must be zero, and so \(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ}\) must induce the zero map in homology.
	
	But, although indeed \(\can^{\bZ}\circ a \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) induces an assignment \(C \to (C\ot_{\bZ} \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_{\bZ} C)^{t\cyc_p}\) for each \(C\in\cD(\bZ)\), it remains to show that it is an actual map in \(\cD(\bZ)\). The key subtlety is that \(\can^{\bZ}\circ a \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) is a map in \(\mathsf{Sp}\), but not necessarily a map in \(\sH\bZ\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}\).
	
	We just proved that this is not true in \(\cD(\Fp)\), nevertheless, we do not have a counterexample nor a disproof for the claim in \(\cD(\bZ)\).

\end{remark}
