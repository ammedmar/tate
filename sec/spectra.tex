\section{The spectral Tate diagonal}

In this section we assume familiarity with the stable \(\infty\)-category of spectra \(\mathsf{Sp}\) and its symmetric monoidal product \(\wedge=\wedge_\bS\).

\subsection{The spectral tate diagonal}

In [Nikolaus-Scholze], they define the following.

\begin{definition}
	Let \(X\) be a spectrum.
	Let \(T^{\mathsf{Sp}}_p\) denote the functor \(\mathsf{Sp} \to \mathsf{Sp}\) given by
	\[
	T^{\mathsf{Sp}}_p(X) \coloneqq (X^{\wedge p})^{t\cyc_p} = \cofib(Nm_{\cyc_p} \colon (X \wedge \overset{p}{\dots} \wedge X)_{h\cyc_p} \to (X \wedge \overset{p}{\dots} \wedge X)^{h\cyc_p}),
	\]
	the Tate construction of \(X^{\wedge p}\), with \(\cyc_p\)-action given by permuting the factors.
\end{definition}

They prove that it is an exact functor and give

\begin{proposition}
	For any \(F \in \Fun^{\mathrm{Ex}}(\mathsf{Sp},\mathsf{Sp})\), evaluation at the sphere spectrum \(\bS\) induces an equivalence
	\[
	\mathrm{Map}_{\Fun^{\mathrm{Ex}}(\mathsf{Sp},\mathsf{Sp})}(\id_{\mathsf{Sp}},F) \longrightarrow \Hom_{\mathsf{Sp}}(\bS,F(\bS)) = \Omega^\infty F(\bS).
	\]
\end{proposition}

Thus, the space of natural transformations \(\id_{\mathsf{Sp}} \to T_p^{\mathsf{Sp}}\) is equivalent to \(\Hom_{\mathsf{Sp}}(\bS,T_p^{\mathsf{Sp}}(\bS)) = \Omega^\infty T^{\mathsf{Sp}}_p(\bS)\).
This allows them to define

\begin{definition}\label{dspectraltate}
	The \defn{spectral Tate diagonal} is the natural transformation \(\Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}} \colon \id_{\mathsf{Sp}} \to T_p^{\mathsf{Sp}}\) that corresponds to the composition
	\[
	\bS \to \bS^{h\cyc_p} \to \bS^{t\cyc_p} = T_p^{\mathsf{Sp}}(\bS).
	\]
\end{definition}


\subsection{\(\sH\Fp\)-modules}\label{HFpmod}

The derived category \(\cD(\Fp)\) is equivalent to the subcategory \(\sH\Fp\text{-}\mathrm{Mod} \subset \mathsf{Sp}\) of \(\sH\Fp\)-modules, or \(\sH\Fp\)-linear spectra, via the Eilemberg-MacLane functor
\[
\sH \colon \cD(\Fp) \longrightarrow \sH\Fp\text{-}\mathrm{Mod},
\]
that sends a chain complex \(V\) in \(\cD(\Fp)\) to the spectrum \(\sH V = \bigvee_i X_i\), where each \(X_i\) is the Eilemberg-MacLane spectra with \(\pi_i X_i = H_i(V)\) and \(\pi_jX_i = 0\) if \(i \neq j\).\martin{Or is it better to say \(\sH V = \bigvee_i \Sigma^i\sH(H_i(V))\)? The thing is that \(\sH\) is used to define \(\sH\).}
On the other direction, the functor
\[
\pi_* \colon \sH\Fp\text{-}\mathrm{Mod} \longrightarrow \cD(\Fp)
\]
takes a \(\sH\Fp\)-module \(X\) to the chain complex with \(\pi_i(X)\) in degree \(i\) and zero differential.

\(\sH\) is a strong symmetric monoidal functor with respect to the tensor products \(\ot_\Fp\) of \(\cD(\Fp)\) and \(\ot_{\sH\Fp}\) of \(\sH\Fp\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}\), that is, there is an isomorphism
\[
\sH V \ot_{\sH\Fp} \sH V \xra{\cong} \sH(V \ot_\Fp V).
\]
Also, the forgetful functor \(\sH\Fp\text{-}\mathrm{Mod} \to \mathsf{Sp}\) has a lax symmetric monoidal structure given by the canonical projection
\[
\sH V \wedge_{\bS} \sH V \to \sH V \ot_{\sH\Fp} \sH V.
\]
% If \(X\) is a \(\sH\Fp\)-module, then \(T_p^{\mathsf{Sp}}(X)\) is also a \(\sH\Fp\)-module.
Their composition induces a (natural) map in \(\sH\Fp\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}\) that Nikoulaus and Scholze denote by \(\can\)\martin{Or rather a map in \(\mathsf{Sp}\) natural with respect to \(\sH \Fp\)-linear maps?}
\[
\can \colon T_p^{\mathsf{Sp}}(\sH V) = (\sH V \wedge_{\bS} \overset{p}{\cdots} \wedge_{\bS} \sH V)^{t\cyc_p} \longrightarrow \left(\sH(V \ot_\Fp \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_\Fp V)\right)^{t\cyc_p}.
\]
Furthermore, both \(\sH\) and the forgetful functor preserve all limits and colimits, so the Tate construction in spectra is compatible to the algebraic version:
\[
\left(\sH(V \ot_\Fp \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_\Fp V)\right)^{t\cyc_p} \cong \sH\left((V \ot_\Fp \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_\Fp V)^{t\cyc_p}\right) = \sH(T_pV),
\]
thus, we will instead write \(\can \colon T_p^{\mathsf{Sp}}(\sH V) \to \sH(T_pV)\).

\subsection{Ring spectra}

Recall that a unitary and commutative ring spectrum (or just a \(\bE_\infty\)-ring spectrum) \(X\) is a \(\bE_\infty\)-algebra in \(\mathsf{Sp}\).
We denote its corresponding homotopy commutative multiplication by \(m_X^{\mathsf{Sp}}\).
If we equip \(X\) with the trivial \(\cyc_p\)-action, \(m_X^{\mathsf{Sp}} \colon X \wedge \overset{p}{\cdots} \wedge X \to X\) is a \(\cyc_p\)-equivariant map of \(\bE_\infty\)-ring spectra.

\subsection{The Tate valued Frobenius}

As an application of the spectral Tate diagonal, in [Nikoluas-Scholze, \S IV], they study the Tate valued Frobenius, and prove that it recovers Steenrod operations.

\begin{definition}
	Let \(X\) be a \(\bE_\infty\)-ring spectrum.
	The \defn{Tate valued Frobenius} on \(X\) is the \(\bE_\infty\)-ring map \(\varphi_X\) given by the composition
	\[
	X\xra{\Delta_p^{t, \mathsf{Sp}}} (X \wedge \overset{p}{\cdots} \wedge X)^{t\cyc_p} \xra{(m_X^{\mathsf{Sp}})^{t\cyc_p}} X^{t\cyc_p}.
	\]
\end{definition}

By further exploring properties of the spectral Tate diagonal, they reach the conclusion that the tate valued Frobenius factors as a \(\bE_\infty\)-map through homotopy fixed points \((X^{t\cyc_p})^{h\Fp^\times}\), and give the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Nikolaus-Scholze, IV.1.15]\label{Tfrobenius}
	The tate valued Frobenius \(\varphi_{\sH \Fp} \colon \sH \Fp \to (\sH \Fp^{t\cyc_p})^{h\Fp^\times}\) is given as a map of spectra by the product of all Steenrod squares
	\[
	\Sq^i \colon \sH \Ftwo \to \sH \Ftwo[i], \quad i\geq 0,
	\]
	for \(p=2\) and by the product over all Steenrod powers
	\[
	\Pow^i \colon \sH \Fp \to \sH \Fp [i(p-1)], \quad i\geq 0
	\]
	and
	\[
	\beta \Pow^i \colon \sH \Fp \to \sH \Fp [i(p-1)+1]
	\]
	for \(p\) odd, where \(\beta\) denotes de Bockstein.
	
	The Tate valued Frobenius \(\varphi_{\sH \bZ} \colon \sH \bZ \to (\sH \bZ^{t\cyc_p})^{h\Fp^\times}\) is given by the product over all \(\Sq^{2i} r\) for \(p=2\) and by the product over all \(\Pow^i r\) for odd \(p\), where \(r \colon \sH \bZ\to \sH \Fp\).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Relation with the algebraic Tate diagonal}

In this section we will show that the spectral Tate diagonal  \(\Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) from \cref{dspectraltate} induces the algebraic Tate diagonal \(\Delta^t_p\).
The composite
\[
\sH V\xra{\Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}} (\sH V \wedge_{\bS} \overset{p}{\cdots} \wedge_{\bS} \sH V)^{t\cyc_p} \xra{\can} \sH\left((V \ot_\Fp \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_\Fp V)^{t\cyc_p}\right) = \sH (T_pV)
\]
is a map in \(\mathsf{Sp}\) that is natural with respect to \(\sH\Fp\)-linear maps, and so, it induces via \(\pi_*\) a homomorphism \(H_*(V)\to H_*(T_p V)\), natural with respect to \(\Fp\)-linear maps.
It is reasonable to ask whether this homomorphism is an algebraic Tate diagonal, that is, if it is an actual nonzero map in \(\cD(\Fp)\).

\begin{lemma}
	\(\pi_0(\can \circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}(\Fp)) \colon \Fp \to H_0(T_p\Fp)\) is the identity \(\Fp \to \Fp\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	Consider the following commutative diagram, where the square was given in \cref{ecan}.
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tikzpicture}
			\node (HF1) at (-5,1){\(\sH \Fp\)};
			%\node (HF2) at (-5,-1){\(\sH \Fp\)};
			\node (THFF) at (0,1){\((\sH \Fp \wedge_{\bS} \overset{p}{\cdots} \wedge_{\bS} \sH \Fp)^{t\cyc_p}\)};
			\node (HFFT) at (0,-1){\(\sH\left((\Fp \ot_\Fp \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_\Fp \Fp)^{t\cyc_p}\right)\)};
			\node (HFT1) at (5,1){\((\sH \Fp)^{t\cyc_p}\)};
			\node (HFT2) at (5,-1){\(\sH (\Fp^{t\cyc_p})\)};
			%
			\draw[->] (HF1)--node[above]{\(\Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}(\sH \Fp)\)}(THFF);
			%\draw[->] (HF2)--node[above]{\(\sH(\Delta_p^t)\)}(HFFT);
			\draw[->] (THFF)--node[above]{\((m_{\sH \Fp}^{\mathsf{Sp}})^{t\cyc_p}\)}(HFT1);
			\draw[->](HFFT)--node[above]{\(\sH \left ((m_{\Fp})^{t\cyc_p}\right )\)}node[below]{\(\cong\)}(HFT2);
			%\draw[double, double distance=2pt](HF1)--(HF2);

	The upper row is the Tate valued Frobenius \(\varphi_{\sH \Fp}\), which induces on \(\pi_0\) the operation \(\Sq^0\) for \(p=2\) and \(\Pow^0\) for odd \(p\) by \cref{Tfrobenius}.
	In both cases the induced homomorphism is the identity \(\Fp\to \Fp\).
\end{proof}

Thus, by \cref{DFpclassification}, we conclude:

\begin{corollary}
	There exists \(a\in\Fp^\times\) such that \(\pi_*(\can \circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}(\Fp)) = a \Delta_p^t\) as natural transformations \(\id_{\Fp}\to T_p\).\martin{I think I can show that \(a\) must be \(1\) using monoidality.}
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}\label{rnonlinearity}
	Notice that \(\can \circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) is not \(\sH \Fp\)-linear, as the above diagram shows, since the upper row is the Frobenius \(\varphi_{\sH \Fp}\), and a consequence of \cref{Tfrobenius} is that it is not \(\sH \Fp\)-linear.
	
	This implies that there is no \(a\in\Fp^\times\) such that \(\can \circ a \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}} = \sH(\Delta^t_p)\), for \(\can \circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) is a map in \(\mathsf{Sp}\) but not in \(\sH \Fp\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}\).
	Nevertheless, \(\pi_*(\can \circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}(\Fp))\) is indeed a map \(\Fp \to T_p\Fp\) in \(\cD(\Fp)\). 
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}
	Let \(T_p^\bZ\) denote the endofunctor of \(\cD(\bZ\)) given by \(T_p^\bZ(C)= (C\ot_{\bZ} \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_{\bZ} C)^{t\cyc_p}\).
	Theorem III.1.10 in [Nikolaus-Scholze] states the following.\martin{what is the right way of quoting the theorem if we are about to say that it is not yet a theorem?}
	\begin{quote}
		\begin{theorem}[No-go theorem]
			Every natural transformation \(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ} \colon \id_{\cD(\bZ)} \to T_p^\bZ\) of functors \(\cD(\bZ)\to\cD(\bZ)\) induces the zero map in homology.%in homology or on homology?
		\end{theorem}
	\end{quote}
	Observe that the existence of algebraic Tate diagonals is a counterexample of this no-go theorem in \(\cD(\Fp)\).
	Yet, the proof of the theorem translates directly to \(\cD(\Fp)\).
	This is only possible because there is a gap in the proof, which was pointed out to the author by Federico Cantero Morán.
	
	In the proof they use the version of \cref{DFpclassification} for \(\cD(\bZ)\), which applied to the functor \(T_p^{\bZ}\) gives
	\begin{quote}
		\begin{theorem}
			Evaluation at the chain complex \(\bZ\in\cD(\bZ)\) induces an equivalence
			\[
			\mathrm{Map}_{\Fun^{\mathrm{Ex}}(\cD(\bZ),\cD(\bZ))}(\id_{\cD(\bZ)},T_p^{\bZ}) \longrightarrow \Hom_{\cD(\bZ)}(\bZ,T_p^{\bZ}(\bZ))\cong \Hom_{\cD(\bZ)}(\bZ,\Fp).

		\end{theorem}
	\end{quote}
	This classifies all possible natural transformations \(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ}\).
	
	Then, they claim that \(\can^{\bZ}\circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) induces on \(\pi_0\) the canonical projection \(\bZ\to\Fp\).
	Indeed, the Tate valued Frobenius \(\varphi_{\sH \bZ}\) is the upper row of the diagram below, and \cref{Tfrobenius} states that it induces on \(\pi_0\) precisely \(\id_{\Fp}\circ r\), where \(r\) is the reduction modulo \(p\). But then \(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ} \coloneqq \pi_*(\can^{\bZ}\circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}})\) is already a counterexample of the no-go theorem, since the induced map on homology is not the zero map.

			\node (HF1) at (-5,1){\(\sH \bZ\)};

			\node (HFFT) at (0,-1){\(\sH\left((\bZ \ot_\bZ \overset{p}{\cdots} \ot_\bZ \bZ)^{t\cyc_p}\right)\)};
			\node (HFT1) at (5,1){\((\sH \bZ)^{t\cyc_p}\)};
			\node (HFT2) at (5,-1){\(\sH (\bZ^{t\cyc_p})\)};

			\draw[->] (THFF)--node[above]{\((m_{\sH \bZ}^{\mathsf{Sp}})^{t\cyc_p}\)}(HFT1);
			\draw[->](HFFT)--node[above]{\(\sH \left ((m_{\bZ})^{t\cyc_p}\right )\)}node[below]{\(\cong\)}(HFT2);

	Instead, they conclude that for every \(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ}\), the underlying transformation of spectra \(\sH(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ})\) factors as \(\can^{\bZ}\circ a \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\), for some \(a\in\Fp\).
	This is a crucial step, since it is later proved that the scalar \(a\) must be zero, and so, any \(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ}\) must induce the zero map in homology.
	
	But that is not the correct conclusion.
	The true consequence is that for every \(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ}\), there is \(a\in\Fp\) such that \(\pi_*(\can^{\bZ}\circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}) = a \Delta_p^{t, \bZ}\), or equivalently that \(\sH (\pi_*(\can^{\bZ}\circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}})) = a \sH(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ})\), if you prefer the version in \(\sH\bZ\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}\).
	
	The key subtlety is that the map of spectra \(\can^{\bZ}\circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) is not the same as the map of \(\sH \bZ\)-modules \(\sH (\pi_*(\can^{\bZ}\circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}))\), since the former is not \(\sH\bZ\)-linear.
	We just showed in \cref{rnonlinearity} that this is what happens in \(\sH\Fp\), and the same argument works for \(\sH\bZ\): the upper row in the diagram above is the Frobenius \(\varphi_{\sH\bZ}\), which is not \(\sH\bZ\)-linear.
		
	Thus, it is true that the unique way for some \(\sH(\Delta_p^{t, \bZ})\) to factor as \(\can^{\bZ}\circ a \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}}\) is that \(a=0\), but what this really means is that the only map that achieves such factorisation is the zero map.
	Meanwhile, the remaining \(p-1\) nonzero natural transformations \(\Delta_p^{t,\bZ} \colon \id_{\bZ} \to T_p^{\bZ}\) are given by \(a \pi_*(\can^{\bZ}\circ \Delta_p^{t,\mathsf{Sp}})\), for \(a\in \Fp^\times\).
		

\end{remark}
